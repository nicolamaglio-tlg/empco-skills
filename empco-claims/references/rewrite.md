# Rewriting flagged claims

The goal is safer alternatives, not a guarantee. A rewrite only lowers risk if the evidence behind it exists, so every alternative has to say what evidence it needs.

## For each flagged claim

- **Original** — exact wording.
- **Problem** — one line, carried over from the assessment.
- **Alternatives** — two or three, ordered from safest to most ambitious. Typical shapes:
  - *Specific and scoped:* name the attribute, the part of the product it applies to, the metric, and the baseline. "Made with [X]% recycled polyester (by weight, excluding trims)" instead of "sustainable materials".
  - *Qualified:* the claim plus the qualification that has to travel with it — same place, same prominence, not a footnote three scrolls away.
  - *Remove it:* sometimes the right answer. A generic claim ("eco-friendly", "green", "climate friendly") with no recognised excellent environmental performance behind it, or a product-level "climate neutral" claim based on offsetting, has no safe rewording.
- **Evidence needed** — for each alternative, what the business would need to hold, and where relevant publish.
- **Residual risk** — what could still be challenged even after the rewrite.

## Rules

- Never invent facts, figures, certifications, or methodologies. Use placeholders like `[X]%` or `[certification scheme]` for anything the user has to supply.
- Don't swap one vague term for another ("eco" → "conscious", "sustainable" → "responsible"). That moves the problem, it doesn't fix it.
- Keep the brand's voice where you can, and write in the original language; offer a translation if useful.
- **Future commitments** ("net zero by 2040"): the rewrite should point to a detailed, realistic implementation plan with measurable, time-bound interim targets, independently verified and publicly available — and say plainly if no such plan is visible.
- **Comparisons** ("50% less water"): state what it's compared to, the method, and the date of the data.
- **Offsetting:** separate what the business reduced in its own value chain from what it financed outside it. Contributing to a project can be described; it can't be turned into a claim that the product is neutral, reduced, or positive in emissions.
- **Nature claims:** keep claims inside the evidence. "Restores biodiversity" becomes, for example, "Funds habitat restoration across [X] ha at [site], monitored by [method]" — with outcomes described as measured, not assumed.

End every set of rewrites with a note that they are drafts for legal review, not cleared copy.
